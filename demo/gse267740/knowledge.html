The question of an analysis question

Draft Fix the question before the method: the cells, the reference, the readout, the replicate and the claim (HJ2, U2). The unit an effect is tested over units

Draft Name the grouping first, such as cells within mice and mice within batches (U2). By default (Pertura's choice, changeable), test by pseudobulk across biological replicates where 3 or more hold both target and control cells, else Wilcoxon on cells against the control cells of the target's own samples; a run can put the sample in the test or sum by sample (U1, U5, PF2, C.genes). Other ways: a mixed model for a nested design, or a permutation over exchangeable units for a small gene set (U2). Lanes, captures and random splits of one pool are technical replicates, not biological ones (PF1, U1). State what n counts; animals pooled before sequencing give pooled cells (PF1, U1, A.what_was_done). Only biological replicates that agree show reproducibility (F.replicates_agree). Combine replicates' p-values by the claim: Fisher's method for an effect in at least one, the largest p for one in all (V4).

Sources: Squair et al. 2021 (tests on cells gave false discoveries that pseudobulk by biological replicate did not; more replicates reduced them, more cells made them worse); Barry et al. 2024 (tests on single-cell CRISPR screens gave excess false positives against non-targeting guides); Milling et al. 2024 (in vivo T cell screens, where many recovered cells can come from a few clones); Pertura's choice: by default, pseudobulk where three replicates hold both sides, else Wilcoxon on cells against the control cells of the target's own samples.

Batches and covariates batches

Draft Consider a batch covariate where control guides are spread unevenly over batches that differ in expression, for a confounder fixed before the perturbation, checked first on control-against-control comparisons (U3, C.genes). Other ways: normalise each gene within a lane against its control cells, or adjust each cell against its nearest control cells, where the variation removed is not the perturbation's effect; then check whether the perturbation shifts the adjusted states, and vary the settings (PF14, U6, C.genes). If target and control cells sit in separate batches, no covariate can separate the perturbation from the batch (U3). A score computed from expression suits as a covariate only if the perturbation does not change it; otherwise the adjustment removes part of the effect (U4).

Sources: Replogle et al. 2022 and Adamson et al. 2016 (each lane normalised to its own control cells); Barry et al. 2024 (the replicate as a covariate improved calibration without making it perfect); Christensen et al. 2026 (adjusting for a variable downstream of the perturbation changes what is estimated); Papalexi et al. 2021 (each cell adjusted against its nearest control cells, with the states' shares checked apart).

The reference a target is compared with controls

Draft By default (Pertura's choice), the reference is the cells carrying control guides from the target's own samples; the alternatives are all control cells pooled, the control_cells choice of C.any_effect, or all other perturbed cells (R5, PF14, PF10, C.any_effect). Cells and guides

The rule that calls each cell's guides guide-calls

Draft Calling rules (a fixed UMI count, a share within the cell, a mixture model per guide) trade purity against coverage: strict calling gives stronger knockdown in fewer cells, loose calling more cells with more contamination, and the best rule differs between screens (G1). Compare rules on cells assigned, knockdown, discoveries and the control guides' false discoveries, and check the chosen rule on control-against-control comparisons, not on hits (G1). If calls do not match the design, such as more cells with several guides than the infection rate predicts, find the cause before changing the calling model (G2). Calls on one or two guide UMIs may be stray guide RNA; B.guide_calls_and_their_evidence reports their share by sample, and a stricter rule can be kept as a cell set (B.guide_calls_and_their_evidence). Take processed calls as given, and say so (G1).

Sources: crispat (Bioinformatics 2024: compared calling rules across screens and technologies, tuned its thresholds to the most significant targets, and fitted its model on the non-zero counts after diagnosing the calls); Pertura's choice: rules judged on control-against-control comparisons, not on hits, and calls on one or two UMIs reported by sample, with no threshold.

Whether each guide changes its own target efficacy

Draft Read each guide's target gene beside its effects: a target's lack of effect means little where its guides move nothing, and a guide short of the line can still act (B.efficacy, PF5). By default (B.efficacy's threshold, changeable), the line is a 30% knockdown under CRISPRi and a rise by the same factor under CRISPRa; other screens use 50%, or for CRISPRa a significant rise or a ratio of mean UMIs (B.efficacy). Under knockout the mRNA can stay, so a flat target gene reads as "transcript unchanged"; the evidence is then a downstream signature, the protein, the edits at the cut site, or the guides agreeing (PF7, G3). A target gene detected in too few cells is not measurable (B.efficacy, ES3). The target gene's own change shows that the guide acts, not what the gene does (PF6). Read apart the perturbation's evidence, its downstream phenotype and the share of cells that respond: a guide short of the line whose phenotype its target's other guides share acts (T3, PF7).

Sources: Replogle et al. 2022 (kept the perturbations knocked down by at least 30%); Zhu et al. 2026 (excluded 869 inefficient guides); Tuladhar et al. 2019 (about half of CRISPR-edited cell lines made unexpected mRNAs or proteins from the targeted gene); Meyers et al. 2024 (a downstream signature as the evidence that a knockout guide worked); Pertura's choice: the 30% line as a changeable default, and under CRISPRa a rise by the same factor.

Draft By default (Pertura's choice), read all the cells assigned to a target; Mixscape's perturbed cells from B.escape are an option, the one published screens take when they discard the rest (B.escape, S3). Whether a target's guides agree guides-agree

Draft Compare a target's guides with each other and against pairs of control guides (F.guides_agree, T1). Two independent guides that agree make an off-target effect unlikely; a guide with no knockdown dilutes the pooled average (F.guides_agree, G4). Pertura's guides check holds a finding where at least two guides go its way and none is significant the other way (T1, F.guides_agree). Where guides disagree, Pertura reads them one by one and keeps the disagreement; published screens drop guides by their correlation, or tier a finding by how many guides support it (G4, F.guides_agree, PF18). Never pick the guide with the largest effect, since that effect is inflated (G4, PF18). Guides of different targets that look alike may share a seed or off-target activity (G5).

Sources: Zhu et al. 2026 (filtered on the correlation between a target's two guides); Alda-Catalinas et al. 2024 (tiered findings by the number of guides that support them); Göring et al. 2001 (the largest of many estimates is inflated); Pertura's choice: two agreeing guides with none against, and the disagreement kept in the record rather than dropped or tiered.

Draft Guides of one target can differ in strength, so pooling them mixes treatments; estimate the effects guide by guide (B.dose, PF8). Enough cells for a target cell-numbers

Draft By default (Pertura's choice, changeable), test the targets with at least 10 cells, and count how many that leaves; published screens set minimums from 5 to 400 cells, and some downsample to equal cells before comparing effect sizes (C.genes, A.what_was_done, T6). The cells per sample and kind of call are the denominator of every comparison (A.what_was_done). Few cells give noisy effects and many give tiny significant ones, so give the effect size and each side's cells (PF16). Read an effect in halves of the data only where they are separate data, such as halves of the samples or, in vitro, of the cells; in vivo a guide's cells can be clones (T6, PF3). A change not seen in few cells means not enough cells, not no change (C.within_states, PF16).

Sources: Replogle et al. 2022 (a minimum of 25 cells; downsampled to equal cell numbers before comparing effect sizes); Nadig et al. 2025 (counts of significant genes agreed across two screens far less than an estimate of the transcriptome-wide effect); Pertura's choice: 10 cells per target as a changeable default, and halves of the data only where they are separate data.

A guide's cells that are a few clones clones

Draft In vivo, a guide's cells can descend from a few transduced cells, so one clone can carry a whole effect (U5, PF3). The field reads each guide's cells against its share of the library, caps a perturbation's cells at twice the median, or counts clones by a UMI or lineage tag in the vector (PF3, B.clonality). A finding seen with two guides is not one clone's (PF3, F.guides_agree). B.clonality gives the share of a group's cells held by its most common guide, with no cut-off; published screens cut at twice the median or at a knee in the vector's UMIs (B.clonality). In T cells that are not TCR-transgenic, read receptor V genes among a target's changed genes as clonality, and leave them out of profiles and clustering (PF4).

Whether a target changes the overall expression profile any-effect

Draft By default (Pertura's choice), C.any_effect tests each target's whole profile by the energy distance from the control cells of its own samples, with a permutation test, and runs control-guide groups through it (C.any_effect, PF14, R1). Published screens also use Mixscape's classes (B.escape), the number of changed genes (C.genes), Hotelling's T² on principal components or a classifier, the last two outside the modules (C.any_effect, B.escape, C.genes). Cell numbers, depth or sick cells also move the whole profile, so read each distance beside the target's UMIs per cell against the control cells', and check its genes at matched depth (C.any_effect, T7, ES1). Where most targets differ from the control cells, test the response they share (D.common_response, R3). The first look's overview reads each target's distance without the permutation test, against control-guide groups drawn to the target's own number of cells, since a small group reads a larger distance from its few cells alone (A.screen_overview, PF16).

The genes a target changes genes

Draft By default (Pertura's choice, changeable), C.genes tests targets with at least 10 cells and calls a gene changed at q < 0.05, with an optional effect-size cut, by the design's test: pseudobulk by replicate where 3 or more hold both sides, else Wilcoxon on cells against the control cells of the target's own samples (C.genes, PF2). Whether a test is calibrated on this screen calibration

Draft Before using a test on many targets, run it on groups of control guides taken as targets (R1, T2, C.genes). Other checks: a target's count of changed genes against the control groups' counts, or a QQ plot of targeting against non-targeting p-values (T2). If the test calls control groups too often, change the covariates or the QC, and check again before any discovery (R1, T2). Build the null from control guides, not from genes assumed unaffected, since an effect away from the target gene can be real (R2). Try a new method for the failure it addresses (R1). If calibration cannot be met, consider stopping inference with that test, rather than looking for one that gives significance (R1).

Sources: Barry et al. 2024 (traced a test's failures on control-against-control pairs to few cells expressing the gene and a replicate confounded with guide frequency, and fixed that mechanism); Alda-Catalinas et al. 2024 (ran the test on cells with non-targeting guides); Zhao 2025 (QQ plots of targeting against non-targeting guides' p-values); Pertura's choice: no inference with an uncalibrated test.

The scope of multiple testing multiple-testing

Draft State how many tests a result ran, which were corrected together, any selection before the test, and the unit of discovery; a correction controls false discoveries only among the tests it covers (C.genes, C.any_effect). By default, C.genes corrects by Benjamini-Hochberg within each target's genes, and C.any_effect across the targets it tests (C.genes, C.any_effect). C.states_and_composition and C.programs correct across every target and state or program they test together (C.states_and_composition, C.programs). Running C.genes on the targets C.any_effect calls selects targets before their genes are tested (C.any_effect, C.genes). A count of changed genes over the selected targets is then controlled by neither correction; report each target's discoveries with its family, or correct for the selection (ES4, C.genes, C.any_effect).

Sources: Benjamini and Bogomolov 2014 (families of tests selected on the data need a correction for the selection as well); Peterson et al. 2016 (correcting within each family alone left the families with discoveries with a very large false discovery proportion); Pertura's choice: each module's scope of correction, stated with its result.

A response every perturbation shares shared-response

Draft By default (Pertura's choice), D.common_response compares each target with the other targets' cells as well as the control cells; targets that still differ have effects of their own (D.common_response, PF9). Another baseline is the mean of all perturbed cells, Systema's reference (R3, PF9). An effect that does not exceed the shared shift is not specific to the target, but the shift can be real biology, so record it as the shared response (R3). Cas9's cuts, stress or a cell-cycle arrest can give such a response (PF9). With few targets, the control guides stay the main reference (D.common_response, PF10). What the expression measures, and what normalisation keeps normalisation

Draft Scaling each cell to its total makes a value a gene's share of that cell's RNA, and removes a difference in total RNA along with the depth (U7). C.genes' edgeR-QL reads raw counts summed by sample; the other tests and modules read log counts per 10,000 or counts scaled to each cell's total (C.genes, C.any_effect, C.states_and_composition, B.efficacy). Units, replicates, batches and covariates

U1. Replicates behind the cells

Questions it answers: The question of an analysis · The unit an effect is tested over · Cell states, and the share of them a target's cells fill · An effect that differs between conditions or cell types

  • If you see: many significant genes in a cell-level test, more with more cells, and units (mice, donors) that differ from each other, so a control-vs-control comparison also gives hits.
  • Consider: summing counts per real experimental unit (pseudobulk by mouse or donor) and testing across units. Random pseudo-replicates (cells split at random into groups) are not units.
  • It acts on: the unit and the estimator. Say what a pseudobulk sums over.
  • Read it as: judge the choice by the null. Control groups, or control guides tested as targets, should give few hits, as in Zhu et al. 2026, Alda-Catalinas et al. 2024 and Schnitzler et al. 2024. In the source, a bulk method run on cells lost its advantage, and so did pseudobulks of random cell groups. Only real replicates restored it. Lanes of one pool are technical replicates, not separate animals.
  • Stop or turn when: consider reporting the result as a description of the pooled cells when the biological replicates cannot be told apart (animals pooled before sequencing). Say so, with the n. Do not present technical splits as biological replicates.
  • Source: Confronting false discoveries in single-cell differential expression, Nat Commun 2021, doi:10.1038/s41467-021-25960-2. Pertura's choice: pooled animals reported as pooled cells.

U2. Cells nested in samples, samples in studies

Questions it answers: The question of an analysis · The unit an effect is tested over · Cell states, and the share of them a target's cells fill

  • If you see: a design with more than one level (cells within mice, mice within experiments or batches).
  • Consider: naming the levels before choosing a method:
    • a simple design suits pseudobulk with a count model;
    • a nested one, a mixed model;
    • a small gene set, a permutation over the right units.
  • It acts on: the correlation structure and the estimator. A method's name is not a design.
  • Read it as: benchmark recommendations are priors for the method, not proof on this screen. A permutation is valid only over exchangeable units. Total cells are not biological power.
  • Stop or turn when: consider closing the question as cannot_answer when the design is fully confounded (a condition is all in one batch). No method recovers that.
  • Source: *Single-cell differential expression analysis between conditions within nested settings*, Brief Bioinform 2025, doi:10.1093/bib/bbaf397. Pertura's choice: full confounding is cannot_answer.

U3. A batch term, and why it is there

Questions it answers: Batches and covariates · Activation and inhibition of one gene

  • If you see: control guides spread unevenly over batches, lanes or replicates, with expression differences between those batches.
  • Consider: a covariate for that batch, only for a confounder fixed before the perturbation (assignment to lane, sequencing run). Check its effect on control-vs-control comparisons first.
  • It acts on: the covariates. Say which kind of confounding you suspect.
  • Read it as: a control guide's null result is a diagnostic, not proof that nothing is confounded. In the low-MOI test paper, adding the replicate improved calibration but did not make it perfect. A permutation-based test was used beside it.
  • Stop or turn when: consider cannot_answer, or a new question at the level where they overlap, when perturbed and control cells do not overlap in the batch the comparison needs (no positivity). The comparison is then not identifiable.
  • Sources:
    • Causal effect estimation from trans-regulatory single-cell CRISPR screens, Cell Genomics 2026, doi:10.1016/j.xgen.2026.101251;
    • *Robust differential expression testing for single-cell CRISPR screens at low multiplicity of infection*, Genome Biol 2024, doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03254-2;
    • Pertura's choice: no overlap is cannot_answer.

U4. Covariates computed from expression

Questions it answers: The question of an analysis · Batches and covariates · What the expression measures, and what normalisation keeps · An effect that follows read depth or cell state · The cell cycle, and whether to adjust for it

  • If you see: a division-phase score, a state score or a response score that differs between target and controls, and a wish to adjust for it.
  • Consider: first whether the score is upstream or downstream of the perturbation.
    • Downstream, it may be a mediator or a collider. Adjusting for it changes the question: the effect beyond that state, not the total effect.
    • The division phase can be noise or biology.
  • It acts on: the covariates and the estimand.
  • Read it as: decide from what the question asks, not from a principal component's correlation. Report which effect the adjusted model estimates.
  • Stop or turn when: consider a new question, with its own population and comparison, when the question becomes the effect within a state.
  • Sources:
    • Causal effect estimation from trans-regulatory single-cell CRISPR screens, Cell Genomics 2026, doi:10.1016/j.xgen.2026.101251;
    • Best practices for single-cell analysis across modalities, Nat Rev Genet 2023, doi:10.1038/s41576-023-00586-w;
    • Pertura's choice: a within-state effect is a new thread.

U5. In vivo, many cells from few clones

Questions it answers: The unit an effect is tested over · A guide's cells that are a few clones

  • If you see: an in vivo screen where a guide's cells may come from few transduced clones (clonal expansion), so recovered cells and reads overstate the independent clones.
  • Consider: estimating coverage from barcode or clone occupancy where the deposit has it. Keep clones, guides, pools, mice and technical lanes apart.
  • It acts on: the coverage and the unit.
  • Read it as: many recovered cells can come from a handful of clones, so one clone can carry a whole effect. Technical lanes do not add biological replicates. A barcode-level test alone gives no inference across mice.
  • Stop or turn when: consider reporting the design's limit (not_enough_data) when clone coverage is too low for the question. Do not use technical lanes to raise the n.
  • Source: Framework for in vivo T cell screens, J Exp Med 2024, doi:10.1084/jem.20230699. Pertura's choice: too few clones is not_enough_data.

U6. Nuisance variation that control cells share

Questions it answers: Batches and covariates · The cell cycle, and whether to adjust for it

  • If you see: variation that control cells show as well as perturbed ones (replicate, division phase, stress), blurring the perturbation's signal.
  • Consider: a local adjustment against control cells, for example subtracting the mean expression of each cell's nearest control cells. Then check apart whether the perturbation changes the shares of those states, and how the result depends on the adjustment's settings.
  • It acts on: the expression signal (a nuisance adjustment), with composition checked apart.
  • Read it as: remove a variation only when it is not the perturbation's effect. Check the shares, and vary the settings. The adjustment would partly erase a perturbation that changes cell division.
  • Stop or turn when: consider the composition as a finding of its own when the perturbation shifts the shares of the adjusted states.
  • Source: *Characterizing the molecular regulation of inhibitory immune checkpoints with multimodal single-cell screens*, Nat Genet 2021, doi:10.1038/s41588-021-00778-2. Pertura's choice: shifted shares are their own thread.

U7. Normalisation to each cell's total

Questions it answers: What the expression measures, and what normalisation keeps

  • If you see: a target whose cells have more or fewer UMIs than the control cells, or many genes moving both ways after normalisation, where the perturbation could change how much RNA a cell makes.
  • Consider: what the normalisation measures. Scaling each cell to its total gives a gene's share of the cell's RNA, and removes a difference in total RNA along with the depth. Spike-ins added in proportion to the cells, or cell counts, measure total RNA, with caveats of their own.
  • It acts on: the quantity measured: a share of the cell's RNA, not an amount per cell.
  • Read it as:
    • the scaling factors cannot tell technical biases from real differences in total mRNA content, and UMIs do not correct for a cell's mRNA content (the first source);
    • in bulk data, cells with high c-Myc made 2 to 3 times more RNA. Normalised to equal totals, some genes seemed to rise and others to fall; normalised to cell number, over 90% of the genes rose (the second source);
    • matching depth, or depth as a covariate, estimates the effect beyond total RNA. Where the perturbation moves total RNA, that is a changed question.
  • Stop or turn when: consider reporting the UMIs per cell beside the effect, with both readings open, when the screen has no spike-ins or cell counts.
  • Sources:
    • Normalizing single-cell RNA sequencing data: challenges and opportunities, Nat Methods 2017, doi:10.1038/nmeth.4292;
    • Revisiting global gene expression analysis, Cell 2012, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2012.10.012;
    • Pertura's choice: UMIs per cell reported beside the effect.

References, control guides and nulls

R1. Calibrate before discovering

Questions it answers: The unit an effect is tested over · Whether a target changes the overall expression profile · The genes a target changes · Whether a test is calibrated on this screen · The gene programs a target moves · How to read one target's evidence · A choice that could change a reading

  • If you see: a test about to be used on many targets, on a screen where it has not been checked.
  • Consider: a fixed order:
    1. define the discoveries the test will be asked for;
    2. run the same test on control-vs-control comparisons (control guides taken as targets: C.genes' calibration check);
    3. if it calls them too often, change the covariates or the QC, and check again;
    4. only then run the discoveries.
  • It acts on: the estimator and its QC, fixed before the results are seen.
  • Read it as: in the source, a test's failure on control-vs-control pairs was traced to a cause: few cells expressing the gene, or a replicate confounded with guide frequency. The fix targeted that cause. Try a method for a calibration failure it addresses, not for its novelty.
  • Stop or turn when: consider stopping inference with that test (the explanation weakened, for the route) when the calibration cannot be met, rather than looking for a test that comes out significant.
  • Source: Robust differential expression testing for single-cell CRISPR screens at low multiplicity of infection, Genome Biol 2024, doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03254-2. Pertura's choice: inference waits for a calibrated test.

R2. A null built from control guides

Questions it answers: Whether a test is calibrated on this screen · A factor's known targets

  • If you see: effects of a target on genes other than its own, and a wish to call them false because they are not "on target".
  • Consider: a null built from control guides against control guides, not from target-gene pairs assumed to be null. A downstream effect can be real.
  • It acts on: what the calibration evidence is.
  • Read it as: control-vs-control comparisons show the test's false calls on negative controls. Real targets' false calls behave the same only if the null is comparable (same sizes, units, selection), a further assumption. An off-target-looking effect is a hypothesis to test, not an error by definition. A value read against control-guide groups is an empirical p. With k of n groups as extreme, p = (k + 1) / (n + 1), at best 1 / (n + 1), as Phipson and Smyth 2010 give it for random permutations. Here n counts groups that share no control guide, not draws.
  • Stop or turn when: consider another question when the question becomes direct versus indirect regulation. A mean expression change alone does not answer it.
  • Source: *Robust differential expression testing for single-cell CRISPR screens at low multiplicity of infection*, Genome Biol 2024, doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03254-2; *Permutation P-values should never be zero*, Stat Appl Genet Mol Biol 2010, doi:10.2202/1544-6115.1585. Pertura's choice: n counts groups sharing no guide.

R3. The shift every perturbation shares

Questions it answers: The question of an analysis · Whether a target changes the overall expression profile · The genes a target changes · A response every perturbation shares · Targets whose profiles look alike

  • If you see: a target's effect on a gene that many other targets show too (a shared shift between perturbed and control cells: cutting, stress, selection).
  • Consider: comparing the effect with what perturbations do to that gene in general (D.common_response: each target against the other targets' cells), and with a simple baseline such as the mean of all perturbed cells.
  • It acts on: the interpretation and, if the claim is narrowed, the comparison.
  • Read it as:
    • an effect that does not exceed the shared shift is not specific to the target;
    • a shared shift can be real biology, so it need not be noise either.

In the source's evaluations, simple perturbed-mean baselines matched or beat complex models, because much of what the models captured was this shift.

  • Stop or turn when: consider narrowing the claim to what is specific, or recording it as the shared response (the explanation weakened, for the specific explanation), when the claim only restates the shared shift.
  • Source: *Systema: a framework for evaluating genetic perturbation response prediction beyond systematic variation*, Nat Biotechnol 2025, doi:10.1038/s41587-025-02777-8. Pertura's choice: a claim that restates the shift is narrowed.

R4. Checking your own method on planted effects

Questions it answers: A method of one's own

  • If you see: a new or custom analysis whose error rate you want to check before trusting it.
  • Consider: planting known effects in real counts (binomial thinning of control cells) rather than simulating from a parametric model. Real counts keep the unwanted variation you worry about.
  • It acts on: the check of the method, not the screen's analysis.
  • Read it as: a check covers only the structures the planting kept (effect sizes, sparsity, confounding). Good results with random labels do not carry over to a confounded design. Thinning lowers counts, so it plants decreases directly. Increases need another construction.
  • Stop or turn when: consider stopping when the planted scenarios fixed in advance have run. Do not keep adjusting until the real screen looks significant.
  • Source: Data-based RNA-seq simulations by binomial thinning, BMC Bioinformatics 2020, doi:10.1186/s12859-020-3450-9. Pertura's choice: stop after the planned scenarios.

R5. Explicit controls, not the rest of the panel

Questions it answers: The reference a target is compared with

  • If you see: a target judged against the other targets of a focused panel (its rank among them), not against control guides.
  • Consider: explicit control guides as the reference: non-targeting guides, or intergenic guides for the cutting response.
  • It acts on: the reference and the hypothesis.
  • Read it as: against the rest of the panel, an effect means "different from these other targets". Changing the panel changes the answer. Against cutting controls, a knockout's effect excludes the response to cutting itself.
  • Stop or turn when: consider saying which comparison is left (cannot_answer for the other) when the screen has no control guides of the needed kind.
  • Source: Framework for in vivo T cell screens, J Exp Med 2024, doi:10.1084/jem.20230699. Pertura's choice: the missing comparison is cannot_answer.

choices/guides.md

Guide calling, guide efficacy and combining guides

G1. Calling rules trade purity against coverage

Questions it answers: The rule that calls each cell's guides

  • If you see: a choice between guide-calling rules (a fixed UMI count, a share within the cell, a mixture model per guide), or a screen deposited with guide counts as well as calls.
  • Consider: comparing calling rules on several measures together:
    • cells assigned;
    • the target's own knockdown;
    • total discoveries;
    • false discoveries of control guides.
  • It acts on: the assignment, not the downstream test.
  • Read it as: strict calling gives stronger knockdown in fewer cells. Loose calling gives more cells with more contamination. The source tuned its thresholds to maximise the significant targets. The number of hits alone does not show a good call, so check a rule chosen that way on control-vs-control comparisons. The best rule differed between screens and technologies.
  • Stop or turn when: consider taking the assignment as given, and saying so, when the deposit holds only processed calls. The assignment cannot be redone (not_enough_data for questions that depend on it).
  • Source: Guide assignment in single-cell CRISPR screens using crispat, Bioinformatics 2024, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae535. Pertura's choice: hits alone do not judge a rule.

G2. Calls that do not match the design

Questions it answers: The rule that calls each cell's guides · Guides that travel together on one vector

  • If you see: more cells with several guides than the infection rate (MOI) predicts, high background counts, or control guides with many false hits.
  • Consider: in this order:
    1. inspect the cells with several guides;
    2. test whether background contamination explains the control failures;
    3. only then change the calling model (in the source, a fit on non-zero counts only).
  • It acts on: the assignment, through a diagnosis.
  • Read it as: go from an anomaly to its cause to a targeted change. This is not a search for more hits.
  • Stop or turn when: consider stopping when the cause is found and fixed, or cannot be. Left unfixed, the affected guides' results are not_enough_data.
  • Source: Guide assignment in single-cell CRISPR screens using crispat, Bioinformatics 2024, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae535. Pertura's choice: unfixed guides are not_enough_data.

G3. A knockout whose target gene does not fall

Questions it answers: Whether each guide changes its own target

  • If you see: a knockout guide whose target mRNA does not decrease.
  • Consider: not reading it as a failed guide. A knockout's mRNA can escape decay, so on-target expression does not measure a knockout's success, nor give a truth for judging calling rules.
  • It acts on: the interpretation of efficacy.
  • Read it as: check efficacy by another route where there is one (protein, a known downstream effect, another guide). Without one, efficacy is unknown, not absent.
  • Stop or turn when: consider not_enough_data when efficacy cannot be shown and the question needs it. This is not evidence of no effect.
  • Source: Guide assignment in single-cell CRISPR screens using crispat, Bioinformatics 2024, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae535. Pertura's choice: unshown efficacy is not_enough_data.

G4. Guides of one target that disagree

Questions it answers: Whether a target's guides agree · Guides of one target that differ in efficacy

  • If you see: a target's guides with different effect sizes or directions.
  • Consider: treating the guides as possibly different treatments before pooling them. Compare them guide by guide, and check each guide's knockdown, cells, and seeds shared with other guides.
  • It acts on: how guides are combined, and what "the target's effect" means.
  • Read it as:
    • pooled guides estimate an average over guide efficacies, and a guide with no knockdown dilutes it;
    • pool where the guides agree, as one source checked before merging;
    • picking the guide with the largest effect to stand for a target inflates the reported effect.
  • Stop or turn when: consider keeping the conflict in the record, rather than merging it into one gene-level result (the explanation weakened, for a single-effect explanation), when neither efficacy nor off-target activity explains the disagreement. Published screens drop or tier such guides instead: some filter on the correlation between a target's guides, some tier a finding by how many of its guides support it.
  • Sources:
    • Causal effect estimation from trans-regulatory single-cell CRISPR screens, Cell Genomics 2026, doi:10.1016/j.xgen.2026.101251;
    • Systematic reconstruction of molecular pathway signatures using scalable single-cell perturbation screens, Nat Cell Biol 2025, doi:10.1038/s41556-025-01622-z;
    • Comprehensive transcription factor perturbations recapitulate fibroblast transcriptional states, Nat Genet 2025, doi:10.1038/s41588-025-02284-1;
    • Pertura's choice: an unexplained conflict is kept, not merged.

G5. Guides of different targets that look alike

Questions it answers: Whether a target's guides agree · Targets whose profiles look alike

  • If you see: guides of different targets with similar phenotypes, or guides that cluster together.
  • Consider: a shared seed sequence or off-target activity before biology. Check the guides' seeds and their on-target activity. Mask each target gene before clustering guides by phenotype.
  • It acts on: which guides are admitted, and how the mechanism is read.
  • Read it as: co-clustering that rests on the target genes themselves, or on a shared seed, is a suspect guide effect, not a robust biological grouping. Selecting guides because several agree makes a later consistency check depend on that selection.
  • Stop or turn when: consider keeping the finding as a candidate, and naming the guide question (a candidate, not validated), when an off-target explanation remains open.
  • Source: Comprehensive transcription factor perturbations recapitulate fibroblast transcriptional states, Nat Genet 2025, doi:10.1038/s41588-025-02284-1. Pertura's choice: open off-target questions keep a candidate.

choices/responses.md

Weak, continuous or mixed responses

S1. A graded response

Questions it answers: Cells that carry a guide but did not respond (non-perturbed cells)

  • If you see: a target's cells spread along a gradient of response (common under CRISPRi), so a split into responders and non-responders fits badly.
  • Consider: modelling the response as continuous: a per-cell perturbation score as a weight or a term in the model, with an explicit rule for targets whose response is too weak to score.
  • It acts on: what "the response" is. A score is not a measured truth of editing.
  • Read it as:
    • record where the score came from, and that it was learned on the same data;
    • when testing genes that went into the score, leave them out of it;
    • a continuous model suits graded responses. It is not right for every modality.
  • Stop or turn when: consider stopping the scoring and falling back to the unweighted analysis when too few genes respond to build the score. The source did so, and did not call the perturbation ineffective (not_enough_data for that target's graded response).
  • Source: *Systematic reconstruction of molecular pathway signatures using scalable single-cell perturbation screens*, Nat Cell Biol 2025, doi:10.1038/s41556-025-01622-z.

S2. Selecting cells by their response

Questions it answers: The question of an analysis · Cells that carry a guide but did not respond (non-perturbed cells) · A choice that could change a reading

  • If you see: a wish to keep only the cells where the perturbation "worked" (strong knockdown, a high score), or to drop low responders.
  • Consider: that this changes the population. The result then holds for the cells that responded, selected by an outcome of the perturbation.
  • It acts on: the population. It is another question.
  • Read it as:
    • a population chosen in advance (a cell type, a state fitted without perturbation labels) differs from one chosen by response after the perturbation;
    • the two answer different questions;
    • write down which one the thread makes;
    • published screens do make this selection: those that say what they do with the cells Mixscape calls not perturbed discard them.
  • Stop or turn when: consider a question of its own, beside the analysis of all the target's cells, when the selection is by response. Its results say which cells they read. A fixed test that selects the same cells does not make the two one question. A selection by response treats control cells, which have no perturbation, differently from targets.
  • Source: Causal effect estimation from trans-regulatory single-cell CRISPR screens, Cell Genomics 2026, doi:10.1016/j.xgen.2026.101251. Pertura's choice: a selection by response is a thread.

S3. Cells with a guide but no response

Questions it answers: Cells that carry a guide but did not respond (non-perturbed cells) · The genes a target changes · How to read one target's evidence

  • If you see: cells carrying a target's guide that look like controls (escapees: in-frame edits, no effective knockdown).
  • Consider: classifying each cell against the control cells, with a mixture whose not-perturbed component is the controls. The source removed the cells classed not perturbed before its downstream analyses, because keeping them would dampen each knockout's effects. By default, read all the assigned cells, with the perturbed class as an option. Each result says which cells it reads.
  • It acts on: the classification of the response, and with it the population.
  • Read it as:
    • the share classed not perturbed is not the editing failure rate. An edit without a detectable expression shift is classed not perturbed, and cell death changes the apparent rate;
    • a class defined by the outcome makes the downstream effect depend on that selection.
  • Stop or turn when: consider not_enough_data when no expression shift separates the classes, rather than forcing a perturbed class. This does not mean the gene has no function.
  • Source: *Characterizing the molecular regulation of inhibitory immune checkpoints with multimodal single-cell screens*, Nat Genet 2021, doi:10.1038/s41588-021-00778-2. Pertura's choice: all assigned cells by default.

choices/programs_and_gene_sets.md

Programs, factors, gene sets and enrichment

PG1. Genes that move together

Questions it answers: The gene programs a target moves

  • If you see: many genes moving together under several targets, with weak single-gene evidence.
  • Consider: a program-level analysis: a gene set fixed beforehand, or a factor model whose factors are associated with the perturbations. Subtract what control guides do to the factors.
  • It acts on: the resolution of the outcome. Keep program claims and gene claims apart.
  • Read it as:
    • a factor's association with a target does not mean every gene loading on it is affected;
    • posterior inclusion probabilities and local false sign rates are not p-values;
    • the number of factors is a modelling choice. Check its effect, and do not choose it for the richest result.

In the source, permuting the perturbation labels showed how much the model wrongly borrowed from them.

  • Stop or turn when: consider fixing the program's genes and testing it on guides or samples it was not found on, or fitting it on the control cells only, when the program was found on the cells it would be tested on. Published screens also test programs found on the screen's cells against the control cells with batch covariates, sometimes with a control-against-control check.
  • Source: *A new Bayesian factor analysis method improves detection of genes and biological processes affected by perturbations in single-cell CRISPR screening*, Nat Methods 2023, doi:10.1038/s41592-023-02017-4. Pertura's choice: test a program on cells it was not found on.

PG2. A score that uses the genes it is tested on

Questions it answers: The gene programs a target moves

  • If you see: a response score or program score built from genes that you then test one by one.
  • Consider: leaving the tested gene out of the score when testing it. Check the score against permuted labels.
  • It acts on: the dependence between the score and the outcome.
  • Read it as: leaving one gene out removes the most direct circularity, not every selection effect. Say that the score was learned on the same data.
  • Stop or turn when: consider stopping the scoring and falling back to the unweighted analysis, as the source did, when too few genes respond to give a stable score. That is not evidence that the perturbation failed.
  • Source: *Systematic reconstruction of molecular pathway signatures using scalable single-cell perturbation screens*, Nat Cell Biol 2025, doi:10.1038/s41556-025-01622-z.

PG3. Overlapping gene sets

Questions it answers: What a list of changed genes means · The regulators of a program, and the axes between programs

  • If you see: pathway gene sets that share many genes, so a target seems to move several pathways at once.
  • Consider: gene sets made exclusive (shared genes removed), labelled as such, and checked against an independent dataset where one exists.
  • It acts on: the program's definition. The score now measures something narrower; say so.
  • Read it as: a shared gene that responds to many pathways is not evidence for any one of them.
  • Stop or turn when: consider stopping when the exclusive sets are too small to score.
  • Source: *Systematic reconstruction of molecular pathway signatures using scalable single-cell perturbation screens*, Nat Cell Biol 2025, doi:10.1038/s41556-025-01622-z. Pertura's choice: stop at sets too small to score.

PG4. Enrichment

Questions it answers: What a list of changed genes means

  • If you see: a list of hits to interpret with gene sets.
  • Consider:
    • the background as the genes the assay detected, not the genome;
    • a correction for the many sets tested;
    • the database's version, recorded;
    • the right test for the input: an over-representation test for a hit list, a rank-based test for a full ranking.
  • It acts on: the interpretation.
  • Read it as: a whole-genome background changes over-representation results substantially. Never change the background, the database or the test family to reach a wanted pathway. An enrichment is a candidate, not a finding.
  • Stop or turn when: consider stopping the formal interpretation when the background, ranking or version cannot be recovered.
  • Source: Urgent need for consistent standards in functional enrichment analysis, PLoS Comput Biol 2022, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009935. Pertura's choice: without the background, ranking or version, the reading stays informal.

PG5. Clusters and their markers

Questions it answers: Cell states, and the share of them a target's cells fill · A change within a state, beside a shift between states

  • If you see: states found by clustering, and markers tested between those clusters.
  • Consider: treating the marker p-values as descriptive. Clusters and markers come from the same data, so the p-values are inflated.
  • It acts on: the interpretation of exploratory labels.
  • Read it as: a significant cluster marker is not independent evidence. A change in how many cells sit in a state (composition) is a different outcome from a change in expression. It needs its own analysis (C.states_and_composition).
  • Stop or turn when: consider fitting the states without perturbation labels when the question is about the states themselves, and recording composition questions and within-state questions apart.
  • Source: Best practices for single-cell analysis across modalities, Nat Rev Genet 2023, doi:10.1038/s41576-023-00586-w. Pertura's choice: composition and within-state questions kept apart.

PG6. A program worth interpreting

Questions it answers: The gene programs a target moves · The regulators of a program, and the axes between programs

  • If you see: programs found by a factorisation or a clustering, about to be named and interpreted.
  • Consider: checking their stability (bootstrap consensus, a varied factor number), and comparing them with independent references or other contexts before giving them a biological name.
  • It acts on: the program's discovery and its interpretation.
  • Read it as: a stable program that matches an independent reference supports a name. A pathway name on an unstable factor is a label, not an explanation. Program discovery and differential expression are different tasks with different outputs.
  • Stop or turn when: consider not reporting it as a program (the explanation weakened) when the program is not stable across resamples.
  • Source: Comprehensive transcription factor perturbations recapitulate fibroblast transcriptional states, Nat Genet 2025, doi:10.1038/s41588-025-02284-1. Pertura's choice: an unstable program is best not reported as a program.

choices/effect_sizes.md

Effect sizes, counting hits, and transcriptome-wide impact

ES1. Hit counts that track cells and depth

Questions it answers: Whether a target changes the overall expression profile · An effect that follows read depth or cell state · How large an effect is, beside how many genes pass · Targets whose profiles look alike

  • If you see: targets or conditions compared by how many genes reach significance, when they differ in cells, depth or noise.
  • Consider: asking for a different quantity: the distribution of true effects, estimated from each gene's effect and its standard error (for example a transcriptome-wide impact), rather than the number of hits.
  • It acts on: the quantity asked. A transcriptome-wide impact is not another hit list.
  • Read it as:
    • more hits with more cells is power, not stronger biology;
    • the estimate needs reliable standard errors and the right replicate units. In vivo, resampling cells does not replace animals;
    • enrichment run on only the significant genes carries a winner's curse.
  • Stop or turn when: consider stopping the comparison of distributions, and fixing the estimate first, when the inputs' standard errors or the replicate design are not trustworthy. Never choose a DE route because its impact comes out larger.
  • Source: Transcriptome-wide analysis of differential expression in perturbation atlases, Nat Genet 2025, doi:10.1038/s41588-025-02169-3. Pertura's choice: in vivo, resampled cells do not replace animals.

ES2. Few true effects among many genes

Questions it answers: A choice that could change a reading · A method of one's own

  • If you see: a comparison of methods or settings on a screen where only a few genes are truly affected.
  • Consider: precision and recall of the calls (or the false calls on control-vs-control comparisons and the recovery of planted effects), not overall accuracy curves. These look good when nearly everything is null.
  • It acts on: how the choice is judged.
  • Read it as: a benchmark's metric is chosen before its results. Judging a method by the hits it gives on the screen under study is not a benchmark.
  • Stop or turn when: consider stopping when the comparison set fixed in advance has run.
  • Source: Single-cell differential expression analysis between conditions within nested settings, Brief Bioinform 2025, doi:10.1093/bib/bbaf397. Pertura's choice: stop after the planned comparison set.

ES3. Sparse genes and the tests that need expression

Questions it answers: Whether each guide changes its own target · The genes a target changes · What a result that detects nothing says

  • If you see: a test that is unreliable for genes detected in few cells.
  • Consider: a detection filter fixed before the test, the same for every group. Say that conclusions do not cover the filtered genes.
  • It acts on: the gene universe.
  • Read it as: a gene the filter removed is untested, not unaffected. A filter chosen on the group itself changes the genes between groups, so the group and its null are not compared on the same genes.
  • Stop or turn when: consider not_enough_data for the gene's question, with that test, when the gene is below the filter.
  • Source: Single-cell differential expression analysis between conditions within nested settings, Brief Bioinform 2025, doi:10.1093/bib/bbaf397. Pertura's choice: a filtered gene's question is not_enough_data.

ES4. Discoveries counted over selected targets

Questions it answers: The scope of multiple testing

  • If you see: discoveries counted or compared over targets that a first test selected (the genes of the targets an any-effect test called), or q-values from corrections of different scopes put side by side.
  • Consider: saying each correction's family (a target's genes, the targets tested) and the selection before it. Where the claim is about the selected families together, adjust for the selection, as the first source does.
  • It acts on: the error rate the reported discoveries carry, and the unit of discovery.
  • Read it as: a correction within each family controls the false discoveries within that family, not over the families a test selected. The second source simulated association tests grouped by variant, with the first source's approach. Correcting within each family alone controlled the average over all families. Yet the families of the discovered variants tended to have a very large false discovery proportion.
  • Stop or turn when: consider reporting each target's discoveries with its family, not one rate over the selected targets, when the claim is a count over selected targets and no correction for the selection ran.
  • Sources:
    • Selective inference on multiple families of hypotheses, J R Stat Soc B 2014, doi:10.1111/rssb.12028;
    • *Many phenotypes without many false discoveries: error controlling strategies for multitrait association studies*, Genet Epidemiol 2016, doi:10.1002/gepi.21942;
    • Pertura's choice: without that correction, discoveries are reported per family.

ES5. A result that detects nothing

Questions it answers: What a result that detects nothing says

  • If you see: a target, gene or program not detected, about to be read as having no effect.
  • Consider: which of three it is: untestable (no cells, no reference, no efficacy shown); not detected with a wide interval (imprecise); not detected with a narrow interval around zero. Only the third is evidence of a small effect. It needs a margin fixed in advance and a test against it: two one-sided tests against the margin. They conclude as a 1-2α confidence interval inside the margin does.
  • It acts on: the interpretation of a non-detection.
  • Read it as: a non-significant test, even with its power stated, is not a test of equivalence. For two averages, the source found the two one-sided tests better than this power approach in most cases, and in all cases with a suited level. The margin is a scientific choice, not one read from the results.
  • Stop or turn when: consider keeping the result not_detected, read as imprecise, when no interval or margin is available. Without efficacy or coverage it is not_enough_data.
  • Source: *A comparison of the two one-sided tests procedure and the power approach for assessing the equivalence of average bioavailability*, J Pharmacokinet Biopharm 1987, doi:10.1007/BF01068419. Pertura's choice: without a margin, not_detected is imprecise.

choices/validation_and_stopping.md

Validation, changing the experiment, and when to stop

V1. Trajectories and pseudotime

Questions it answers: A shift along a trajectory or a pseudotime

  • If you see: a target that seems to shift cells along a trajectory or a pseudotime.
  • Consider: checking the trajectory with methods of different assumptions when the topology is unknown, and the kinetics behind any velocity estimate.
  • It acts on: the state process and the model's assumptions.
  • Read it as: an embedding or pseudotime does not show a dynamic or causal process. A shift along it is first a change of states (composition).
  • Stop or turn when: consider stopping the higher-level interpretation, and recording the composition finding as such, when the reference states or the kinetic assumptions are not supported.
  • Source: Best practices for single-cell analysis across modalities, Nat Rev Genet 2023, doi:10.1038/s41576-023-00586-w. Pertura's choice: unsupported, it stays a composition finding.

V2. A candidate nominated by correlation

Questions it answers: The regulators of a program, and the axes between programs · The experiment that would settle a finding

  • If you see: a gene or regulator that correlates with the readout of interest across cells or conditions.
  • Consider: treating the correlation as a reason to nominate the candidate, and testing it by perturbation (here, its own guides) before any causal reading.
  • It acts on: candidate selection and validation.
  • Read it as: correlation nominates; a perturbation tests. A candidate list chosen from correlations is a prior for the screen, not a finding of it.
  • Stop or turn when: consider keeping the candidate for another experiment (a candidate, not validated) when it has no guide in the screen.
  • Source: *Characterizing the molecular regulation of inhibitory immune checkpoints with multimodal single-cell screens*, Nat Genet 2021, doi:10.1038/s41588-021-00778-2. Pertura's choice: without a guide, it stays a candidate.

V3. A clear separation in a supervised plot

Questions it answers: The figure for each kind of evidence

  • If you see: perturbed and control cells cleanly separated in an embedding made after a supervised step (a discriminant analysis on the labels, then UMAP).
  • Consider: reading it as a display of the difference the method was trained to find, not as evidence.
  • It acts on: the interpretation of a figure.
  • Read it as: a supervised projection separates labelled groups by construction, so the separation is not an independent validation. Judge the effect by the test, against controls.
  • Stop or turn when: consider ending the thread without a finding (no new information) when the separation is the only support.
  • Source: *Characterizing the molecular regulation of inhibitory immune checkpoints with multimodal single-cell screens*, Nat Genet 2021, doi:10.1038/s41588-021-00778-2. Pertura's choice: a separation alone is not a finding.

V4. Combining replicates' tests

Questions it answers: The unit an effect is tested over

  • If you see: a target tested in several mice or replicates, and a choice of how to combine their p-values.
  • Consider: choosing the combination by the claim:
    • a liberal combination (Fisher's) tests that not every replicate is null;
    • a conservative one (the largest p) tests that every replicate shows the effect.
  • It acts on: the claim made across replicates.
  • Read it as: these are two claims, not two p-values for one claim, so never pick the one that gives more hits. Replicates without a defined value are left out of the test and counted as such, not set to zero effect. Published screens mostly run one test, on the pooled cells with the replicate as a covariate, or on pseudobulk. Few combine per-condition tests by Fisher's method. The choice arises only where per-replicate tests exist.
  • Stop or turn when: consider not_enough_data, and saying so, when the claim needs every replicate and some lack values.
  • Source: Framework for in vivo T cell screens, J Exp Med 2024, doi:10.1084/jem.20230699. Pertura's choice: missing values for such a claim give not_enough_data.

V5. A thread that has said what it can

Questions it answers: What a result that detects nothing says · When a thread has said what it can

Papers report the final pipeline, not the routes they dropped, so there is no published stopping rule to copy. The cards' stops are Pertura's choices, in the record's terms: signals to weigh, not commands. A question closes as:

  • cannot_answer: a missing unit, reference or readout, or a design with no overlap;
  • not_enough_data: efficacy or coverage not shown.

A thread ends with:

  • the explanation weakened: a diagnostic did not support the explanation;
  • a new question: the population, comparison or outcome changed;
  • a candidate, not validated: candidates waiting for an independent test;
  • no new information: more of the same.

Never end a route only because it gave few hits. Never continue one only because it gives many.

Most choices that matter change the question, not the method. Within one question, the population, the comparison and the quantity are fixed. HJ3. What a published choice changes

Questions it answers: A choice that could change a reading

Published screens make choices that Pertura's defaults do not. The screens that say what they do with the cells Mixscape calls not perturbed discard them. Tian et al. 2019 kept, for its tests, the half of each target's cells with the best on-target knockdown. Such a choice changes the population. The result then holds for the cells that responded, selected by an outcome of the perturbation (PF17). It does not hold for all the cells given the guide. A guide picked to stand for a target because its effect is the largest gives an inflated effect (PF18). Whether the choice is a paper's or Pertura's, a result says which population, reference and guides it rests on, and what each changes.

HJ4. The point to stop

Questions it answers: How to read one target's evidence · What a result that detects nothing says · When a thread has said what it can

Papers mostly report the final pipeline, not the routes they tried and rejected. There is no published stopping rule to copy, so these are Pertura's own. A finding or a thread states its standing in one of these words:

  • detected or not_detected, with the runs it rests on and its figure. A not_detected without an interval or a test against a margin is imprecise, not evidence of no effect (ES5);
  • not_enough_data: coverage or an effective perturbation is not shown. This is not evidence of no effect;
  • cannot_answer: the design is confounded, units are missing, or there is no matching reference or readout. Say what is missing.

A thread ends when it has given what it can. Its reason says which end it reached:

  • the explanation is weakened: a diagnostic or independent evidence does not support it. Record it; the thread can end there. Never drop a route only because it gave few hits;
  • a new question: the population, mechanism, comparison or outcome would have to change. Open it as a thread of its own;
  • a candidate, not validated: the next step is validation or the user's decision. A candidate is still called a candidate;
  • no new information: more of the same would add nothing.

pitfalls.md

Pitfalls

Mistakes that make a Perturb-seq finding look better than it is. Read them before the first analysis. The names B.efficacy, D.common_response and so on are Pertura's modules, under the design's questions A-H.

Samples, replicates and cells

PF1. Say what the n is

Questions it answers: The question of an analysis · The unit an effect is tested over · An effect that differs between conditions or cell types · What a finding says, and what it rests on

Biological replicates (mice, donors, separate infections) and technical replicates (lanes or wells of one pool) are different. A test of differential expression has to account for the variability between biological replicates (Squair et al. 2021). A lane covariate adjusts for differences between lanes. It does not turn lanes into animals. Where animals were pooled before sequencing, a result describes the pooled cells. Say so, with the n.

  • Source: Squair et al., Confronting false discoveries in single-cell differential expression, Nat Commun 2021, doi:10.1038/s41467-021-25960-2. Pertura's choice: lanes are not animals; pooled animals are reported as pooled cells.

PF2. Cells as the observations can give excess false positives

Questions it answers: The unit an effect is tested over · The genes a target changes · An effect that differs between conditions or cell types · What a finding says, and what it rests on

Tests that compare individual cells give false discoveries that tests on pseudobulk (cells summed by biological replicate) do not. Adding replicates reduces them; adding cells makes them worse (Squair et al. 2021). On single-cell CRISPR screens, the existing tests were miscalibrated against non-targeting guides, with excess false positives (Barry et al. 2024). The design chooses the default test: pseudobulk by replicate where 3 or more biological replicates hold both sides; otherwise a test on cells against the control cells of the target's own samples, with pseudobulk by sample as a finding's check where 3 samples hold both sides. Three is Pertura's default and can be changed; a test named for a run needs 2 replicates a side, and its result notes the difference. A finding whose two tests disagree says so.

  • Source: Squair et al., Nat Commun 2021, doi:10.1038/s41467-021-25960-2; Barry et al., *Robust differential expression testing for single-cell CRISPR screens at low multiplicity of infection*, Genome Biology 2024, doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03254-2. Pertura's choice: three replicates by default, and pseudobulk as a finding's check.

PF3. In vivo, a guide's cells can be few clones

Questions it answers: Enough cells for a target · A target's cell numbers as fitness · A guide's cells that are a few clones

A clone makes a guide's cells look abundant and its change look well supported, but its cells are not independent. In vivo screens pass through bottlenecks, clonal competition and random drift. These can obscure the biological signal (Datlinger et al. 2025) and change the rates of false positives and false negatives (Milling et al. 2024). Treat a guide far above its share of the library as expansion until shown otherwise. The cells of TP53's guides rose by clonal expansion, which the plasmid library's even counts showed (Sivakumar et al. 2025, in vitro). Dixit et al. 2016 read a guide's cells against its share of the original library. Read B.clonality's top-guide shares beside B.abundance, against the library's counts where the user has them. Count the clones where the vector carries a UMI (Datlinger et al. 2025; Milling et al. 2024). A finding seen with two guides is not one clone's (F.guides_agree).

  • Source: Datlinger et al., Systematic discovery of CRISPR-boosted CAR T cell immunotherapies, Nature 2025, doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09507-9; Milling et al., Framework for in vivo T cell screens, J Exp Med 2024, doi:10.1084/jem.20230699; Sivakumar et al., *Benchmarking and optimizing Perturb-seq in differentiating human pluripotent stem cells*, Stem Cell Reports 2025, doi:10.1016/j.stemcr.2025.102713; Dixit et al., Cell 2016, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2016.11.038. Pertura's choice: expansion until shown otherwise; two guides as the check against one clone.

PF4. In T cells, clones carry their receptor genes

Questions it answers: A guide's cells that are a few clones

A T-cell clonotype is identified by its V genes and CDR3 sequences (Heumos et al. 2023), so the cells of one clone share their receptor V genes. In T cells that are not TCR-transgenic, read receptor V genes among a target's changed genes as clonality, not as the perturbation's effect. Leave the receptor genes out of the profiles and the clustering. Chung et al. 2026 regressed the TCR genes out during clustering, in TCR-transgenic cells. In TCR-transgenic T cells the receptor cannot mark clones (Milling et al. 2024).

  • Source: Heumos et al., Best practices for single-cell analysis across modalities, Nat Rev Genet 2023, doi:10.1038/s41576-023-00586-w; Chung et al., *Atlas-guided discovery of transcription factors for T cell programming*, Nature 2026, doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09989-7; Milling et al., J Exp Med 2024, doi:10.1084/jem.20230699. Pertura's choice: receptor genes among a target's changed genes are read as clonality.

Knockdown of the target gene

PF5. Many guides do not knock down their target

Questions it answers: Whether each guide changes its own target · Guides of one target that differ in efficacy · How to read one target's evidence

Check the target gene (B.efficacy) before reading a target's lack of effect. Without knockdown, the question cannot be answered for that guide. It does not mean the gene does nothing. In a genome-scale CRISPRi screen, the target fell significantly for 73% of the guides. The guides without a detectable knockdown had a low baseline expression or fewer cells (Zhu et al. 2026). In another screen, about 70% of CRISPRi guides repressed their target by more than half (Sivakumar et al. 2025). Where some perturbations did not knock down their target, the share of perturbations with an effect is a lower bound (Replogle et al. 2022). Published screens set such guides aside. For their functional analyses, Replogle et al. 2022 kept the perturbations with at least 30% knockdown or with the target not detected. Zhu et al. 2026 excluded 869 inefficient guides. In Pertura, the knockdown line is B.efficacy's threshold choice: 30% by default, and changeable.

  • Source: Zhu et al., *Genome-scale perturb-seq in primary human CD4+ T cells maps context-specific regulators of T cell programs and human immune traits*, Cell 2026, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.002; Sivakumar et al., Stem Cell Reports 2025, doi:10.1016/j.stemcr.2025.102713; Replogle et al., Cell 2022, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2022.05.013. Pertura's choice: B.efficacy's line at 30% by default; without knockdown, the question cannot be answered for that guide.

PF6. The target gene moves because of the guide

Questions it answers: Whether each guide changes its own target · Targets whose profiles look alike · A factor's known targets

The target gene goes down under CRISPRi and up under CRISPRa (Christensen et al. 2026). This shows that the guide works (B.efficacy). It is not a finding about the gene's function. When perturbations are compared or clustered, published screens mask the target genes. Replogle et al. 2022 masked them so that the knockdown would not drive the clustering. Zhu et al. 2026 set the on-target z-scores to zero.

  • Source: Christensen et al., Causal effect estimation from trans-regulatory single-cell CRISPR screens, Cell Genomics 2026, doi:10.1016/j.xgen.2026.101251; Replogle et al., Cell 2022, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2022.05.013; Zhu et al., Cell 2026, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.002. Pertura's choice: the target's own change is not a finding about its function.

PF7. A knockout's mRNA often stays

Questions it answers: Whether each guide changes its own target · How to read one target's evidence

A knockout's mRNA can escape nonsense-mediated decay, or the repair can be in frame. Dixit et al. 2016, Otto et al. 2023 and Meyers et al. 2024 say so. In CRISPR-edited cell lines, about half made unexpected mRNAs or proteins from the targeted gene (Tuladhar et al. 2019). A flat target gene after knockout says nothing on its own. In one knockout screen, 64 guides out of 111 lowered their target. The others were ineffective guides, edits that escaped decay but agreed with the target's other guides, or targets expressed little or not at all (Otto et al. 2023). For knockout, the evidence that a guide worked is a downstream signature (Meyers et al. 2024), protein where it was measured, agreement between the target's guides (F.guides_agree), and the edits at the cut site where the reads cover it (Zheng et al. 2024).

  • Source: Dixit et al., Cell 2016, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2016.11.038; Otto et al., Mol Cell 2023, doi:10.1016/j.molcel.2023.03.013; Meyers et al., Haematologica 2024, doi:10.3324/haematol.2023.284901; Tuladhar et al., CRISPR-Cas9-based mutagenesis frequently provokes on-target mRNA misregulation, Nat Commun 2019, doi:10.1038/s41467-019-12028-5; Zheng et al., Cell 2024, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2024.04.050.

PF8. Guides of one target can differ in strength

Questions it answers: Guides of one target that differ in efficacy · Cell states, and the share of them a target's cells fill

B.dose measures it. A guide's efficiency can vary across a target's guides, so treating them as one perturbation mixes different treatments. One remedy is to estimate the effects guide by guide (Christensen et al. 2026). In a genome-scale screen, most disagreements between a target's guides came from their on-target knockdown or the strength of their effects (Zhu et al. 2026). Zheng et al. 2024 tested composition per guide because gRNA performance is known to vary. Analyse by guide. Keep the conflict rather than merging it into one gene-level result.

  • Source: Christensen et al., Cell Genomics 2026, doi:10.1016/j.xgen.2026.101251; Zhu et al., Cell 2026, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.002; Zheng et al., Cell 2024, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2024.04.050. Pertura's choice: the conflict stays in the record.

Rival explanations that are always there

PF9. A response common to the perturbations

Questions it answers: The reference a target is compared with · The genes a target changes · A response every perturbation shares

Cas9's cuts set off a p53-dependent response to DNA damage. With it comes selection against the cells whose p53 works (Haapaniemi et al. 2018; Ihry et al. 2018). A stress response or a cell-cycle arrest can occur across many perturbations alike (Viñas Torné et al. 2025). A target's effect that every target shows is that common response (D.common_response). Compare the target with the other targets' cells as well as with the control cells. Systema moves the reference to the average of the perturbed cells. The targets that still differ have effects of their own. Where the library has them, control guides that cut where no gene is (safe-targeting, intergenic) are the fairer control for the cutting (Morgens et al. 2017; D.cutting_response).

  • Source: Haapaniemi et al., Nat Med 2018, doi:10.1038/s41591-018-0049-z, and Ihry et al., Nat Med 2018, doi:10.1038/s41591-018-0050-6 (in retinal pigment epithelial cells and in stem cells); Viñas Torné et al. (Systema), Nat Biotechnol 2025, doi:10.1038/s41587-025-02777-8; Morgens et al., Nat Commun 2017, doi:10.1038/ncomms15178 (in growth screens).

PF10. Control-guide cells and all other perturbed cells answer different questions

Questions it answers: The question of an analysis · The reference a target is compared with · A response every perturbation shares

Against the control-guide cells, a target's effect is what the target does. Against all other perturbed cells, it is what stands out from the average perturbation. Published screens use both, most often the control cells. The two references give different readings of the same profiles (Viñas Torné et al. 2025).

  • Source: Viñas Torné et al. (Systema), Nat Biotechnol 2025, doi:10.1038/s41587-025-02777-8. Pertura's choice: each reference is read as its own question.

PF11. A shift in the states a target's cells fill is itself an effect

Questions it answers: Cell states, and the share of them a target's cells fill · A change within a state, beside a shift between states · The cell cycle, and whether to adjust for it

A target can change which cell states its cells are in. That shift is itself an effect of the target, and is reported as one. Many published screens show a perturbation's shares of states, most with a test. A change of a gene's mean can then be a change of states. With each cell's state in the model, some guides' effects were accounted for; Dixit et al. 2016 read this as those perturbations mainly changing the subtype proportions. Fit the states without perturbation labels, on all the cells, or on cells without a perturbation with the others placed on them (Dixit et al. 2016). Then compare (C.states_and_composition). Keep a shift of the shares apart from a change within a state. Test within each state (C.within_states), or add each cell's state to the model, as Dixit et al. 2016 did to recover the effects within a state.

  • Source: Dixit et al., *Perturb-seq: dissecting molecular circuits with scalable single-cell RNA profiling of pooled genetic screens*, Cell 2016, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2016.11.038. Pertura's choice: the states are fitted on all the cells.

PF12. Technical causes of a composition shift come first

Questions it answers: Cell states, and the share of them a target's cells fill

Guide-UMI depth that differs between guides can shift the share of cell types a guide's cells fill. Zheng et al. 2024 found a Spearman correlation of 0.84 with one cell type's share, which was gone after down-sampling. The injection and the vector can move the cells they reach into a state. Shen et al. 2026 saw injection injury and AAV transduction move cells toward a less mature oligodendrocyte state. Differences between guides may also reflect the cells collected (Zheng et al. 2024). Check these causes before reading biology.

  • Source: Zheng et al., Cell 2024, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2024.04.050; Shen et al., Nat Commun 2026, doi:10.1038/s41467-026-69677-6.

PF13. A state held by one perturbation may be doublets

Questions it answers: Cell states, and the share of them a target's cells fill

Doublets of two cell types can give wrong cell-type labels (Heumos et al. 2023). In one screen, a cluster made mostly of one target's cells expressed some cell-type markers and markers of its own. The authors read it as not doublets (Zheng et al. 2024). They had also scored doublets in each 10x channel and removed the clusters with high scores. Check such a state's markers, and the doublet calls where they were made, before reading it. The source checked one cluster and gives no general rule.

  • Source: Heumos et al., Nat Rev Genet 2023, doi:10.1038/s41576-023-00586-w; Zheng et al., Cell 2024, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2024.04.050. Pertura's choice: a state held by one perturbation is checked before it is read.

PF14. Targets and control cells from different samples

Questions it answers: The question of an analysis · Batches and covariates · The reference a target is compared with · Whether a target changes the overall expression profile · Cell states, and the share of them a target's cells fill · The cell cycle, and whether to adjust for it · Targets whose profiles look alike · An effect that differs between conditions or cell types

A target is best compared with the control cells of its own samples. Otherwise, a target that sits in one sample also differs by its sample. Replogle et al. 2022 and Adamson et al. 2016 normalise each lane or batch to its own control cells. Published screens pool all the control cells at least as often as they match them by sample. Some compare with all other perturbed cells instead.

  • Source: Replogle et al., Cell 2022, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2022.05.013 ("Within each gemgroup, for each gene, we compute the mean and standard deviation of expression within control cells"); Adamson et al., Cell 2016, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2016.11.048 ("cells were normalized with respect to control cells within the same lane"). Pertura's choice: control cells matched by sample.

PF15. Large fold changes of genes the controls barely express

Questions it answers: The genes a target changes

Where a gene's counts are low, its estimated log fold change varies widely. A large estimate need not be a large difference (Zhu et al. 2019). Shrink the fold changes, or set such genes apart, before ranking genes by effect size.

  • Source: Zhu, Ibrahim and Love, Heavy-tailed prior distributions for sequence count data, Bioinformatics 2019, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/bty895.

PF16. Few cells give noisy effects; many cells give tiny significant ones

Questions it answers: Enough cells for a target · Whether a target changes the overall expression profile · How large an effect is, beside how many genes pass · A change within a state, beside a shift between states · A pair of perturbations against each alone · What a finding says, and what it rests on · How to read one target's evidence · What a result that detects nothing says

A target with few cells has noisy effects. A target with many cells has tiny significant ones. The number of genes that pass a test follows the cells as well as the effect. For the same perturbations in two screens, the number of significant genes agreed far less than an estimate of the transcriptome-wide effect (R² 28.4% against 59.7%; Nadig et al. 2025). Report both the effect size and the cells.

  • Source: Nadig et al., Nat Genet 2025, doi:10.1038/s41588-025-02169-3. Pertura's choice: the cells are reported beside the effect size.

Choices that change the question

PF17. Filtering cells by their response changes the question

Questions it answers: The question of an analysis · Cells that carry a guide but did not respond (non-perturbed cells) · A choice that could change a reading

Keeping only the cells where the knockdown worked, or the cells in one cluster, selects cells by a variable the perturbation itself changes. Conditioning on such a variable biases the estimated effect of the perturbation, even where the variable only mediates the effect (Christensen et al. 2026). Published screens do keep only the cells classed as perturbed. Saputra et al. 2025 removed the cells Mixscape classed as not perturbed before testing genes. Such a selection changes the population, so it is another question. Open it as a thread of its own, beside the analysis of all the target's cells.

  • Source: Christensen et al., Cell Genomics 2026, doi:10.1016/j.xgen.2026.101251; Saputra et al., Mol Oncol 2025, doi:10.1002/1878-0261.70153. Pertura's choice: a selection of cells is read as conditioning, and opened as a thread of its own.

PF18. Do not pick the guide with the largest effect, or combine rules until something passes

Questions it answers: Whether a target's guides agree · A choice that could change a reading

Do not pick the guide with the largest effect, combine significance rules until something passes, or drop replicates that disagree. Flexibility in how data are analysed and reported greatly raises the real false-positive rate. The remedy Simmons et al. 2011 give is to disclose what was done. An effect picked as the largest of many estimates is inflated (Göring et al. 2001). Published screens ask a target's guides to agree instead. Zhu et al. 2026 filtered on the correlation between a target's two guides, each with a significant knockdown (F.guides_agree). The record keeps every run, so what was tried shows.

  • Source: Simmons, Nelson and Simonsohn, False-positive psychology, Psychol Sci 2011, doi:10.1177/0956797611417632; Göring, Terwilliger and Blangero, *Large upward bias in estimation of locus-specific effects from genomewide scans*, Am J Hum Genet 2001, doi:10.1086/324471; Zhu et al., Cell 2026, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.002. Pertura's choice: these points applied to guides, rules and replicates; the record as the disclosure.

PF19. The gene universe of an enrichment is the genes the assay detected

Questions it answers: What a list of changed genes means

Use the genes the assay detected, not the genome. Most genes are not expressed and cannot be called changed, and single-cell data detect only a few thousand. Across seven RNA-seq datasets, a whole-genome background gave results on average only 44% similar to those with the detected genes (Wijesooriya et al. 2022). Use the genes detected at a level where they could be called changed. Sivakumar et al. 2025 used the genes expressed in at least 5% of cells. For an enrichment among the targets that are hits, the background is the targeted genes (Replogle et al. 2022).

  • Source: Wijesooriya et al., Urgent need for consistent standards in functional enrichment analysis, PLoS Comput Biol 2022, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009935; Sivakumar et al., Stem Cell Reports 2025, doi:10.1016/j.stemcr.2025.102713; Replogle et al., Cell 2022, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2022.05.013.

One way through a lead

  1. Read the result with what it rests on: the cells, the samples, the guides, the test.
  2. List the moves it suggests (below), and keep the ones that serve the question.
  3. A trust move, where one is suggested, usually comes first, since the rest rests on it. Then organise and locate, then the design's contrasts. Outside knowledge usually comes last, and it is always recorded beside the finding as prior knowledge, said with the claim it informs. This order is Pertura's suggestion.
  4. Run the move, and say which earlier result it changes.
  5. A lead ends when it is explained as far as the screen can show, when it fails a trust move, or when no move applies. A failed lead is a result too, and is reported as one. Then propose the validation of what is left (the paradigm cards' last step).

Trust, organise and locate moves are the agent's to run. Which leads to follow is worth asking the user. A move that needs outside data or a download waits for the user's approval.

  • Published screens:

T3. Each perturbation acts

Questions it answers: Whether each guide changes its own target

  • Worth considering when: a guide moves its target gene less than the line or not at all, or some cells look unperturbed.
  • Run: guides view own_gene (B.efficacy), then view escape (B.escape), and reproducibility view guides (F.guides_agree). Scope

A screen of regulators changes thousands of genes at once, and a gene-by-gene result is hard to read. These papers first find the programs the cells coordinate, then test which perturbations move each program [Ota 2025, step 6; fibroblast TF states 2025, step 7]. The structure among the regulators then names the lead: an axis of two programs, or sets of regulators acting together [Schnitzler 2024, Fig. 3a,b; astrocyte TFs 2026, step 7].

Another paradigm may fit better when a contrast already defines the outcome, or when the question is about cell states [paradigms_of_deep_perturbseq_analysis.md: P2, P3].

The steps' judgement

P1.1. The programs are a choice of the analysis, not a fact of the data. The same screen gives different programs with different methods and K. Schnitzler ran cNMF for K from 3 to 100 and chose K = 60 by stability and reconstruction error. It then set aside the 10 components that correlated with the 10x lane (r > 0.15) [Schnitzler 2024, Extended Data Fig. 2c-g]. Programs found on the cells they are tested on look stronger than they are. Finding them in the control cells only avoids that [C.programs fails_when; Schnitzler 2024, Supplementary Notes 2-3, the control-cell ablation].

P1.2. The papers named each program before it carried a finding. Schnitzler named its programs from their regulators, motif enrichment, GO enrichment and whether their genes are higher in endothelial cells than elsewhere [Schnitzler 2024, Fig. 1c,d]. Aryal named the myeloid program P-15 by its GO enrichment [Aryal 2026, step 3].

P1.3. A regulator of a program is a perturbation whose cells change that program's usage against the control cells. Schnitzler tested it with MAST against control-guide cells, with the lane as a covariate. It checked the false discovery rate by resampling control cells as targets, which gave no regulators [Schnitzler 2024, Fig. 1c, Extended Data Fig. 3a-c]. C.programs runs the same test on groups of control guides as its calibration, and counts what it calls there [C.programs returns].

P1.5. Grouping the regulators by their effects is the common way to find structure. Published screens correlate and cluster the perturbations' profiles, and some cluster regulators into co-functional sets [coverage map, rows 1 and 28]. In the astrocyte screen, TF families clustered together, which served as the check on the five co-functional modules [astrocyte TFs 2026, step 7]. In the HSPC screen, regulators in the same perturbation cluster were more likely to interact physically (STRING) [HSPC 2026, step 6].

P1.6. An axis is two programs that the regulators move in opposite directions. Few published screens make this move [coverage map, row 47]. It is worth taking when the question is a balance between two programs, or when two programs' effects anticorrelate over the regulators. Upstream and downstream members of the CCM pathway moved the five CAD programs in opposite directions, so the screen read as one signalling axis [Schnitzler 2024, Fig. 3a,b]. The S phase and G2/M programs shared regulators with opposite signs [Ota 2025, step 8].

P1.7. A ranking without a threshold from the control guides gives every target a rank. Schnitzler ran control cells, resampled as if they were targets, through the same procedure that called regulators, and it called none [Schnitzler 2024, analysis_chains_of_sixteen_more_papers.md, the controls column]. pt.rank_on reads each target against the control-guide groups' 95th percentile on the same axis [pt.rank_on].

Forks

P1.1 fork: the source of the programs and how many to keep. Papers found them:

  • in all the screen's cells or its perturbation effects: cNMF on the cells [Schnitzler 2024, Extended Data Fig. 2; Ota 2025, step 6; astrocyte TFs 2026, step 7], a regularised linear model with 17 programs [Aryal 2026, step 3], sparse PCA of the perturbation-induced changes [fibroblast TF states 2025, step 7];
  • in the non-targeting cells first [lncRNA senescence 2026, step 8];
  • in a patient atlas, then scored in the screen [ZEB2 2025, steps 1-2].

Published screens score known gene sets or signatures per perturbation about as often as they find programs in the data [coverage map, rows 3 and 8]. C.programs offers gene_sets hallmark or given, cnmf, genenmf, genenmf_meta (programs recurring across samples) or sklearn_nmf, and fit_on all or controls. For cNMF, K is the most stable of 5 to 15 unless given (Pertura's choice) [C.programs choices].

P1.4 fork: the programs that matter here. Papers chose them:

  • by genetic association: CAD variants enriched in five programs by a Fisher test [Schnitzler 2024, Fig. 2b,c]; loss-of-function burden for a trait [Ota 2025, step 7];
  • by patients: only one of 17 programs held for survival in two AML cohorts [Aryal 2026, step 5]; modules specific to tumour-associated against healthy macrophages [ZEB2 2025, step 1];
  • by disease signatures: GO profiles compared with patient neurons [depression genes 2026, step 9].

The user's question, recorded at the start, decides among these [ADR 0030, the flow].

P1.8 fork: the leads that go to validation. Papers took:

  • the strongest regulators of the relevant programs: TLNRD1 and CCM2 [Schnitzler 2024, Fig. 3, 4];
  • the gene acting on all key programs in one direction with a large loss-of-function effect: SUPT5H [Ota 2025, step 10];
  • regulators converging on one program: the MEN1-DOT1L-KAT6A hub [Aryal 2026, step 3];
  • the one that also passed a rerun under the disease condition: Ferd3l [astrocyte TFs 2026, step 10].

Choosing the leads

A strong lead goes beyond the control-guide groups on the programs that matter, its guides agree, and it holds under the design's other test [pitfalls.md: Rival explanations; P1.8 control]. Among such regulators, one that no earlier study named is the stronger result. TLNRD1 was one of 17 genes, out of 41, that no prior study had nominated [Schnitzler 2024, Supplementary Notes 2-3].

Validation

Papers re-tested the leads one at a time:

  • an arrayed knockdown with bulk RNA-seq [Schnitzler 2024, Fig. 3c,d; Aryal 2026, step 1];
  • the phenotype the programs predict: ACTA2 and collagen for myofibroblast regulators [fibroblast TF states 2025, step 10], drug co-inhibition for the hub [Aryal 2026, step 7];
  • in vivo: xenografts for HSPC regulators [HSPC 2026, step 8], a disease mouse model [astrocyte TFs 2026, step 12].

Connections

  • P5 enters at P1.4 when genetics chooses the programs [Schnitzler 2024, Fig. 2; Ota 2025, step 7; HSPC 2026, step 13; depression genes 2026, step 1].
  • P2 enters at P1.7 when a score built from the programs ranks the regulators: an anti-tumour score from the TAM modules [ZEB2 2025, step 7].

Pitfalls, each with its check

  • A program found on the data it is tested on looks stronger than it is. Check: fit_on="controls", or fixed gene sets, and the calibration on control-guide groups [C.programs fails_when; Schnitzler 2024, the control-cell ablation].
  • A program of one lane or sample read as biology. Check: components that follow a lane set aside, or genenmf_meta's programs recurring across samples [Schnitzler 2024, Extended Data Fig. 2; C.programs fails_when].
  • A shift that every target shares inflates every program effect. Check: the same program against the other targets' cells (reference="other_targets"; D.common_response) [C.programs fails_when].
  • Cells as the observations call too much. Check: what the same test calls in groups of control guides (pt.calibrate) [pitfalls.md: Samples, replicates and cells; Schnitzler 2024, Extended Data Fig. 3].
  • A regulator's absence may be a guide that did not work. Check: B.efficacy before reading a target as no regulator, and each guide alone (res.by_guide()) [pitfalls.md: Knockdown of the target gene].
  • A program change can be a change of which states the cells are in. Check: pt.composition of the states, then the program within one state [pitfalls.md: Rival explanations, composition].

Scope

These papers first fix one axis from a contrast the question defines, then place every perturbation on it, so a screen of many targets reads as one ranking [Sengstack 2026, step 2; Akana 2026, steps 8-9]. The contrast is often in the screen's own design: late against early passage [Sengstack 2026, step 2], co-culture with T cells against monoculture in the control cells [Akana 2026, step 8], a fibrotic against a naive state [Aguado-Alvaro 2025, step 2]. It can also come from outside: patient neurons [depression genes 2026, step 9] or a published polarity score [ZEB2 2025, step 7].

The steps' judgement

P2.1. The contrast is the biological question itself, so it is chosen before any perturbation is read [paradigms_of_deep_perturbseq_analysis.md, P2]. Where the design holds two conditions, the control cells' difference between them is the natural contrast, because it measures the response in this screen's cells without any perturbation [Akana 2026, step 8].

P2.2. Akana built its contrast from the non-targeting cells alone, by edgeR on pseudobulk profiles with the sequencing batch as a covariate, at |logFC| > 0.25 and BH FDR < 0.01 [Akana 2026, step 8]. Sengstack took the late against early passage log fold changes [Sengstack 2026, step 2]. A signature built and scored on the same cells looks stronger than it is; Akana left one guide out, built the signature from the others and scored it in the cells of the guide left out [Akana 2026, step 9].

P2.3. Sengstack scored each perturbation by the correlation of its log fold changes against non-targeting cells with the ageing contrast [Sengstack 2026, step 2]. ZEB2's anti-tumour score was the mean enrichment of the IFN response, antigen presentation and complement programmes minus the immune-suppression and phagocytosis ones [ZEB2 2025, step 7]. C.programs scores the user's or the agent's gene lists per target against the control cells, and runs the same score on groups of control guides as its calibration [C.programs returns]. Where the contrast moves the cell cycle, the score is recomputed within one phase: Sengstack's reversal persisted within a fixed phase, although most hits shifted cells out of G1 [Sengstack 2026, step 3].

P2.4. A ranking needs a line, or every perturbation has a rank. Sengstack drew it at r <= -0.3 [Sengstack 2026, step 2]. The control-guide groups put through the same score give the screen's own line [C.programs returns; pitfalls.md: Samples, replicates and cells].

P2.5. A perturbation's place on the axis can differ by condition. Akana tested each monoculture signature in co-culture and the reverse; IFNB1 acted only in co-culture [Akana 2026, step 9]. Cao read each target's effect in each patient sample, and a circuit was absent where its genes' baseline expression was absent [Cao 2026, step 6]. E.condition_x_perturbation reads each target against each condition's own control cells [E.condition_x_perturbation returns].

P2.6. When the targets are regulators expressed in the cells, each target's changed genes include the other targets, and the screen holds a network among them. Akana kept the regulators whose changes were consistent with their phenotype and found a hub raised by two resistance hits and lowered by six sensitizing hits [Akana 2026, step 12]. The lncRNA screen placed HOTAIRM1 as a central hub of its perturbation network [lncRNA senescence 2026, steps 8 and 10]. A regulator whose own knockdown failed is no node: B.efficacy first [pitfalls.md: Knockdown of the target gene].

Forks

P2.1 fork: Which contrast defines the score? Papers took:

  • a contrast in the design's control cells: co-culture against monoculture [Akana 2026, step 8];
  • a contrast of states the screen's cells pass between: late against early passage [Sengstack 2026, step 2], fibrotic against naive [Aguado-Alvaro 2025, step 2];
  • programmes from a patient atlas combined into one score [ZEB2 2025, steps 1 and 7];
  • patients against controls, as GO profiles [depression genes 2026, step 9].

P2.7 fork: Which of the perturbations past the line go to validation? Papers took:

  • the top score whose arrayed rerun reversed the most hallmarks [Sengstack 2026, step 5];
  • the top score that also agreed with a published score and changed chromatin most [ZEB2 2025, steps 7 and 10];
  • the one whose effect ran against the others' direction [lncRNA senescence 2026, step 10];
  • the cluster most similar to patients, then its top gene [depression genes 2026, step 9].

Choosing the leads

A strong lead's score goes beyond the control-guide groups the way the question asks, its guides agree, and it holds in the condition the question names [P2.7 control; F.guides_agree]. Papers then took the ones an arrayed rerun reproduced: four TFs re-tested one at a time, EZH2 the strongest [Sengstack 2026, step 5].

Validation

  • an arrayed perturbation with the phenotype the score predicts: proliferation, proteasome and mitochondria [Sengstack 2026, step 5]; T cell co-culture and antigen presentation [ZEB2 2025, step 8];
  • the lead in an animal: AAV8-EZH2 in aged mice [Sengstack 2026, step 6]; a targeted siRNA in tumours [ZEB2 2025, step 12].

Connections

  • P1 enters at P2.2 when the score is built from programmes: ZEB2's anti-tumour score from its TAM modules [ZEB2 2025, steps 6-7].
  • P3 enters after P2.4 when the ranked perturbations are read on states: Aguado-Alvaro's top fibrotic-score hits went to Perturb-seq and their shares of states [Aguado-Alvaro 2025, step 3].

Pitfalls, each with its check

  • A signature tested on the cells it was built from looks stronger than it is. Check: build it on part of the guides or cells and score the rest [Akana 2026, step 9].
  • A score that follows the cell cycle. Check: the score within one cell-cycle phase (C.cell_cycle) [Sengstack 2026, step 3].
  • A ranking with no line. Check: the control-guide groups through the same score [C.programs returns].
  • One condition read for all. Check: the score in each condition against that condition's own control cells [Akana 2026, step 9; Cao 2026, step 6].
  • A regulator read as no regulator because its knockdown failed. Check: B.efficacy before the network [pitfalls.md: Knockdown of the target gene].
  • Cells as the observations call too much. Check: what the same test calls in groups of control guides (pt.calibrate) [pitfalls.md: Samples, replicates and cells].

Scope

These papers put two or more perturbations in the same cells: designed pairs with the singles measured alongside [PMC10030154 Wessels 2023, Fig. 4c], one perturbation in every cell with a second varying [PMID41676564 Reisman 2026, Fig. 1F,G], or cells that carry several guides by chance [PMC11035494 Yao 2023, Fig. 5f]. They measure what a pair does that its singles do not. Each pair is set against its singles as a whole profile [PMC6746554 Norman 2019, Fig. 4A-F], then gene by gene [PMC5181115 Dixit 2016, Fig. 5B-E], then on the readouts the question names [PMC10562403 Xia 2023, Fig. 5c-e].

With singles only, an interaction can only be predicted, as an index per pair, and then tested in another experiment [PMC13149302 Aryal 2026, Fig. 3A-B, step 7].

The steps' judgement

P6.1. In one screen the double had 169 cells, against 449 and 400 for its singles [PMC10562403 Xia 2023, Fig. 3g]. So the pair and both singles are counted in the same units first, and control pseudo-pairs are drawn at the pair's cell numbers [E.combinations returns; pt.pair_fit returns]. The fit writes the pair's profile as two weights on its singles' profiles (mean R-squared 0.71) [PMC6746554 Norman 2019, Fig. 4A-F]. The same regression explained about 40% of a deletion's signature from three singles' [PMC10567566 Santinha 2023, Fig. 4f-h].

P6.2. CBL/CNN1 cells ran from unperturbed through the single to the double [PMC6746554 Norman 2019, Fig. 3C-E]. E.similar_targets correlates each pair's profile with each single's [E.similar_targets returns].

P6.3. The weights name the kind: epistasis (KLF1/CEBPA, 0.19 and 0.72), suppression (PTPN12/SNAI1, 0.60 and 0.57), synergy (CBL/CNN1, 1.24 and 0.8) [PMC6746554 Norman 2019, Fig. 4A-F]. Wessels grouped the fits into four interaction clusters; 33 pairs fit well, many members of one complex [PMC10030154 Wessels 2023, Fig. 4g, Ext. Data Fig. 8d-f].

P6.4 and P6.5. SMARCA4/2, SMARCC1/2 and SMARCD1/2 were synergistic; ARID1A/B, GLTSCR1/1L and cross-subcomplex pairs were additive [PMC12593249 Otto 2023, Fig. 5D-F, 6B-E]. E.combinations calls the modes with model regulatory_modes, on a genes step's DE tables [E.combinations choices, inputs].

P6.7. Cells with both guides had a lower state-4 fraction than Cebpb cells [PMC10562403 Xia 2023, Fig. 3g]. C.states_and_composition gives each pair's share of each state against its controls [C.states_and_composition returns].

P6.8 and P6.9 (an anchor). The partner is read against the anchor alone, with a non-targeting partner where the library has one: the paired gRNAs changed 28, 18 and 8 genes on average (positive-hit, negative-hit, non-targeting) [PMID41676564 Reisman 2026, Fig. 1K]. Most genes stayed within 0.3 of the anchor-only response [PMID42146330 Chang 2026, Ext. Data Fig. 6f].

P6.10. Where guides share cells by chance, per-gene interactions lack power. With perturbations gathered into modules, three modules showed intra-module interactions in guide-pooled data, and none did in a larger conventional screen [PMC11035494 Yao 2023, Fig. 5f, Suppl. Fig. 6]. A program modelled on both genes' status showed no synergy [PMID42327190 Lee J 2026, Fig. 4E].

P6.11. Tnnt2 rose with Gata4 alone, and Hcn4 needed Hand2 plus Mef2c. TBX5 induced Hcn4 beside HAND2 or MEF2C alone but repressed it beside both [PMID42146410 Markham 2026, Fig. 3C-F]. PERK/ATF4 had the largest and most specific regulon of the three UPR branches [PMC5315571 Adamson 2016, Fig. 3F].

P6.12. A treatment crossed with a perturbation asks the same question: a KO-by-treatment interaction model (FDR < 0.25) [PMC9732527 Hou 2022, Fig. 6A-B], or an observed-minus-additive synergy score per perturbation [PMC11457336 Liu 2024, Fig. 4H]. E.condition_x_perturbation tests a target's difference between two conditions per program [E.condition_x_perturbation returns].

P6.13. The dominant-interaction genes, not the additive ones, were enriched for co-binding of Nfkb1 and Rela [PMC5181115 Dixit 2016, Fig. 5F].

Forks

P6.1 fork: the expectation that counts as no interaction. Papers took:

  • the singles' combined effect: the average and the product of the singles on one marker [PMC10030154 Wessels 2023, Fig. 4c], or an observed-minus-additive score [PMC11457336 Liu 2024, Fig. 4H];
  • the pair fitted as a weighted sum of its singles' [PMC6746554 Norman 2019, Fig. 4A-F; PMC10030154 Wessels 2023, Fig. 4g];
  • an interaction term per gene in a model over the cells [PMC12593249 Otto 2023, Fig. 5D-F, 6B-E; PMC5181115 Dixit 2016, Fig. 5B-E].

E.combinations offers fitted_weights (the module's default), plain_sum and regulatory_modes [E.combinations choices]. The papers do not settle on one way. The card's default is the weighted sum of the singles, Norman et al.'s fit, as Norman and Wessels read their pairs [PMC6746554 Norman 2019, Fig. 4A-F; PMC10030154 Wessels 2023, Fig. 4g]. It is an analytic choice, not the user's: the agent chooses the model and says why, workers run the other models beside it, and the report says what the choice moves [ADR 0032, D8 and D9].

P6.6 fork: the readouts that matter for this question. Papers chose:

  • one marker, CD11b, or a sorted marker with each partner's effect paired against its effect alone [PMC10030154 Wessels 2023, Fig. 4c; PMID41676564 Reisman 2026, Fig. 1F,G];
  • two readouts of different kinds, a combination counting when high on both: a surface-protein score and an expression program [PMC10562403 Xia 2023, Fig. 5a-e];
  • a program that only two regulators moved [PMID42327190 Lee J 2026, Fig. 4E];
  • the whole profile [PMC6746554 Norman 2019, Fig. 4A-F].

The user's question, recorded at the start, decides among these [ADR 0030, the flow]. E.combinations returns each program's observed and additive score, from hallmark sets or given ones [E.combinations returns, choices].

P6.14 fork: the pairs that go to validation. Papers took:

  • the highest interaction index among regulators of one program: MEN1-KAT6A (synergy index 86) [PMC13149302 Aryal 2026, Fig. 3A-B];
  • the combination high on two readouts: Med12+Cebpb [PMC10562403 Xia 2023, Fig. 5c-e];
  • the partners that converge on one pathway: competing end-joining genes with NBN [PMID42146330 Chang 2026, Fig. 2g].

Choosing the leads

A strong lead is a pair whose departure goes beyond the control pseudo-pairs at its own cell numbers and agrees between units, and whose members both act [E.combinations returns, fails_when; paradigms_of_deep_perturbseq_analysis.md, P6 controls]. One that two kinds of readout both single out is the stronger result [PMC10562403 Xia 2023, Fig. 5c-e].

Validation

Papers re-tested the lead one pair at a time, as with a drug pair for a predicted synergy: Menin and KAT6A inhibitors (ZIP 20.19) [PMC13149302 Aryal 2026, step 7].

Connections

  • P1 comes first when only two regulators move one program [PMID42327190 Lee J 2026, Fig. 4E].
  • P7 comes first when a pooled screen picked the pairs: a fitness interaction [PMC6746554 Norman 2019, Fig. 3C-E], a MAP2-sorted paired screen [PMID41676564 Reisman 2026, Fig. 1F,G].
  • P2 enters at P6.6 when a score ranks the combinations [PMC10562403 Xia 2023, Fig. 5a,b]; P3 enters at P6.7 when the pair is read on cell states [PMC10562403 Xia 2023, Fig. 3g].

Pitfalls, each with its check

  • A member that does not act makes a pair look like its other single. Check: each member's target gene in the pair's cells before epistasis is called [E.combinations fails_when; pitfalls.md: Knockdown of the target gene].
  • A departure can agree between units by chance, more so with few cells, and per-gene interactions lack power. Check: control pseudo-pairs at the pair's cell numbers; a power analysis beforehand; the cells of the pair and of each single beside every call; modules in place of genes [E.combinations fails_when; paradigms_of_deep_perturbseq_analysis.md, P6 controls; PMC11035494 Yao 2023, Fig. 5f; pitfalls.md: Rival explanations, cell numbers].
  • A pair is one construct, so a departure may be its dose. Check: said with each departure; a second construct per pair settles it [E.combinations fails_when; pitfalls.md: Knockdown of the target gene, guides differ in strength].
  • A shift of states read as a change of genes, or a pair-only state that is doublets. Check: pt.composition, then the genes within one state; the state's markers [pitfalls.md: Rival explanations, composition and doublets; PMC10562403 Xia 2023, Fig. 3g].
  • A reference that answers another question. Check: each call names its reference, the control cells for what the pair does, the anchor with a non-targeting partner for what the partner adds [pitfalls.md: Rival explanations, the controls; PMID41676564 Reisman 2026, Fig. 1K].

B.efficacy: Each guide's change of its own target gene

Questions it answers: Whether each guide changes its own target · What the expression measures, and what normalisation keeps · Activation and inhibition of one gene · How to read one target's evidence · What a result that detects nothing says

The biology. Each guide's change of its own target gene, described against a line. Under CRISPRi or CRISPRa it shows whether and how far a guide moves its target: a target's lack of effect means little where its guides move nothing, and a guide short of the line can still act (a partial knockdown), which its downstream phenotype and its target's other guides show. Under knockout the transcript can stay, because it escapes decay or the edit is in frame (PF7). A knockout guide that leaves it is "transcript unchanged". The perturbation's evidence, its downstream phenotype and the share of cells that respond (B.escape) are read apart (T3). Under CRISPRi, Pertura's default line is a fall of 30% or more, the filter of Replogle et al. 2022; other screens use 50%, and the line is a choice (threshold): a description, never a test of whether a guide acts. Published CRISPRa screens call a significant rise (edgeR) or compare mean UMIs, with no fixed factor. Under CRISPRa the default line is a rise by the same factor, 1.43 times (Pertura's choice).

The measurement. Per guide, the target gene's log2 fold change against the reference cells over all the units pooled (the units that hold both the guide's cells and reference cells), with each unit's own row beside it; its detection, and the power to see a halving (a doubling under CRISPRa). A guide is placed by its pooled row, or by the median over its units where no pooled row can be measured; its change in per cent and where it falls against the line. A row needs 10 cells of each side by default (policy, min_cells).

  • Read: ES3, PF15
  • Do: it is best reported as not measurable; the guides' agreement downstream then carries the evidence.

modules/B.escape.md

B.escape: Perturbed and non-perturbed cells within a target's cells

Questions it answers: The question of an analysis · Cells that carry a guide but did not respond (non-perturbed cells) · Whether a target changes the overall expression profile · The genes a target changes · How to read one target's evidence

The biology. Within a target's cells, some cells were changed by the perturbation and some were not (mosaic editing, in-frame edits, weak knockdown in some cells). Mixscape (Papalexi et al. 2021) calls each cell perturbed or non-perturbed against its nearest control cells; Mixscale gives a continuous score. modules/C.any_effect.md

C.any_effect: Each target's effect on the whole profile

Questions it answers: The question of an analysis · The reference a target is compared with · Whether a target changes the overall expression profile · The scope of multiple testing · What the expression measures, and what normalisation keeps · An effect that follows read depth or cell state · How to read one target's evidence · What a result that detects nothing says

The biology. A target's effect on the cells' whole profile. Published screens test it in several ways: Mixscape's classes of a target's cells, Hotelling's T² on 20 principal components, the number of changed genes against the controls', an energy-distance permutation test, or a classifier. Replogle et al. 2022 ran both the energy test and the count of changed genes, and the two calls agreed with a Jaccard index of 78.7%. B.escape gives Mixscape's classes and C.genes the changed genes. For the reference, published screens either pool all the control cells or match them to the target's sample, lane or condition. This module runs the energy distance between a target's cells and the control cells of its own samples, with a permutation test (Pertura's choice). Each side is drawn to at most 1,000 cells, as the overview's distances are (Pertura's choice, for a screen's test in minutes). The distance grows as the cells fall (Peidli et al. 2024), so two targets' distances compare at the same number of cells; the overview reads each against control-guide groups of its own number. A target is tested from 10 cells by default (policy, min_cells).

The measurement. Per target: the energy distance in the PCA of variable genes (pertpy's DistanceTest), its permutation p-value, the Benjamini-Hochberg q, the cells and the cells drawn on each side, and the target's UMIs per cell against the control cells'. The control-guide groups go through the same test, drawn the same way.

Fewer cells (fewer_cells, such as [25, 50, 100, 200]). Published screens down-sample a perturbation's cells to see how many its call needs. Each target with more cells than a number is drawn to it, 3 times, and tested again, its control cells drawn as in the whole test. Benjamini-Hochberg runs over the targets tested at that number. The result gives, per number, the share of the targets called on every cell that are called again, and the targets called only with fewer cells.

Published screens downsample to equal cell numbers before comparing effect sizes, put the genes detected, the mitochondrial share, the depth or the cells per perturbation in the test, or check for a signature of dying cells.

  • Shows in: control_cells (same_samples by default)
  • Read: PF14, U3
  • Do: same_samples is the default; published screens match the control cells to the sample, lane or condition. The alternative is all control cells, which other screens pool.

The biology. The genes each target changes. Most published screens test a target's cells against the control cells, most often with a Wilcoxon test; fewer sum the cells by replicate (pseudobulk). modules/E.combinations.md

E.combinations: Each pair against its singles

Questions it answers: A pair of perturbations against each alone

The biology. Two perturbations together against each alone. The pair's profile is fitted as a weighted sum of its singles' (Norman et al. 2019's regression, with R², the coefficients' magnitude and their asymmetry), or set against their plain sum; what is left is the interaction. Norman 2019, Wessels 2023, Otto 2023, Dixit 2016, Hou 2022 and Santinha 2023 read pairs this way, by Norman's fit or by interaction terms, and report the coefficients and the fit. Otto et al. 2023 found its cells too few to tell additivity from synergy. Yao et al. 2023 found no second-order term even at q < 0.5. None of these papers asks a departure to reproduce between units. The module's default is Norman's weighted sum (model fitted_weights); plain_sum and regulatory_modes are the other choices.

The measurement. The testable pairs: the pair and both singles with min_cells cells (20 by default, a choice), pooled over the units that hold the pair, each cell against its own unit's control cells. Per pair: its cells and its singles', whether each member acts in the pair's cells, the weights, R² and the size of the departure. modules/E.condition_x_perturbation.md

E.condition_x_perturbation: Effects that differ between conditions

Questions it answers: An effect that differs between conditions or cell types

The biology. A perturbation's effect compared between conditions (a stimulus, a time point, a tissue, a cell type). Each effect is read against the control cells of its own sample in the same condition. Liu et al. 2024 runs it against the same condition's control cells and against the untreated ones. Most published screens test cells.

The measurement. Per target and program, the change of the target's effect on the program's score from one condition to the other. By default the test is the design's, as C.genes chooses it (Pertura's choice). Where the design names biological replicates and 3 or more of them hold 10 of the target's cells and 10 control cells in each condition, each unit's difference from its control cells is tested over the units: paired where each unit holds both conditions, Welch otherwise, after Squair et al. 2021. Elsewhere it is one test on cells: least squares of the score with each sample in each condition its own baseline, the target, and the target in the second condition, whose coefficient is the change. The cells, control cells and samples of each condition are stated. A change the layout cannot estimate is left out and counted. Benjamini-Hochberg runs over all the target x program tests. The table by_unit gives, per target, program and unit, the score's difference from that unit's control cells, for the figure. A named test, cells or units, makes one test for every target.

Other readings (what):

  • genes: the named genes' log-normalised expression in place of the scores, by the same test;
  • controls: the control cells' own response, read before the targets as published screens describe it. It gives each gene's change from one condition to the other (scanpy's Wilcoxon on cells), and each protein's where the screen measured them (CLR per cell);
  • shares: each target's share of each state in each condition (cell_states from a C.states_and_composition run), by Fisher's exact test against the control cells of its own samples there. The conditions are set side by side, and the states a target moves in one condition only stand out. This reading is descriptive between the conditions.

Any analysis tool also runs once per condition, tissue or cell type with by, each apart and gathered.

  • Do: as built; the result says which control cells each condition uses (C1).

  • Shows in: the cells and samples of each condition in the table; test in the result; the targets with one sample in a condition, named in the result
  • Read: U1, PF2, V4

modules/E.similar_targets.md

E.similar_targets: Targets with similar profiles

Questions it answers: Targets whose profiles look alike

The biology. Targets with similar profiles. Perturbations of one complex or pathway look alike, so an unknown gene can be placed by the known ones it resembles. Published screens correlate the perturbations' mean profiles, usually by Pearson's correlation (Replogle et al. 2022: "the correlation between mean expression profiles as a scale-invariant metric of similarity"). Replogle 2022, Adamson 2016, Norman 2019 with Wessels 2023, and Akana 2026 correlate profiles z-normalised to the control cells. Replogle 2022 and Zhu 2026 relate only the perturbations with a strong effect. Replogle 2022, Zhu 2026 and Norman 2019 test the clusters against known complexes. This module reads each pair against pairs of control guides (Pertura's choice).

The measurement. The correlation of every two targets' profiles over well-expressed genes, with the targeted genes left out and each cell against its own unit's control cells. By default the profiles are pooled over the units of one kind of capture, and a target needs min_cells cells in all (30 by default, Pertura's min_cells_profile). With profile by_sample, they are correlated within each unit and averaged over the units holding both; a target needs min_cells cells in a unit. Each pair is read against control-guide pairs, and each target's most similar targets are listed. With more than one kind of capture, each kind's correlation and reference stand beside them, with kinds_disagree.

Pairs with opposite effects stand beside the pairs alike: a pair is opposite when it correlates as low as at most 5% of the control-guide pairs. Given a genes run (de), each pair past a line lists the genes both targets change: the same way for a pair alike, opposite ways for an opposite pair. This is why two targets look alike, read in their genes. The pairs whose two target genes share a GO cellular component (a known complex) are set against the other pairs, as published screens check their groups against known complexes. This check is prior knowledge.

modules/G.meaning.md

G.meaning: The gene sets a finding's genes fall in

Questions it answers: What a list of changed genes means · Outside knowledge and outside data

The biology. The meaning of a finding's changed genes: the gene sets they over-represent (up and down apart, against the genes the screen detects), or where a ranking enriches (GSEA). By default the detected genes are the background, as a methods paper recommends (PG4, PF19). Published screens state different backgrounds: the expressed genes, all genes, all screened genes, or the genome against the expressed genes. An enrichment names a pathway, not a mechanism in these cells, so it is prior knowledge. Up and down genes are tested apart by default, as some published screens do (Pertura's choice).

Each target's own genes, on a genes run. With a C.genes run (de), method ora reads each target's changed genes, up and down apart, against the genes tested for that target, as published screens read a perturbation's changed genes (X1); method gsea reads each target's genes ranked by their signed test statistics. q is the package's Benjamini-Hochberg within each list.

Activity, on a genes run. With method ulm on a C.genes run (de), every target is read at once. Each factor's activity (CollecTRI) or pathway's activity (PROGENy) comes from the target's signed test statistics over its tested genes, by decoupler's univariate linear model, as decoupler's tutorials read a contrast by its statistic. The statistic is each gene's z, from its p, with the sign of its change. A sparse gene's extreme fold change with a weak test then weighs little. Each sits beside the control-guide groups' line: the 95th percentile of the same activity in the gene test's own calibration groups. q is decoupler's Benjamini-Hochberg over the sources within each target.

The measurement. Per gene set and direction, the over-representation test or GSEA's NES and FDR, the overlap and its genes, and the universe. The sets are any MSigDB collection by its name (Hallmark by default; GO, Reactome, immune signatures, cell types and the rest), for human or mouse, or one's own (gene_lists: a lab's sets or another library's, any organism). The universe is the genes detected in 1% of cells by default, or the genes given as universe, such as the genes a test tested. A set counts at q < 0.05 by default (q).
